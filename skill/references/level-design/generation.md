# Generation and curriculum

Translate difficulty adjectives into a target metric band and player-profile/seed protocol. Report unsupported controls before generation. Use bounded attempts; preserve attempted, accepted, and rejected counts and reasons. Valid yield uses all attempted candidates as its denominator.

Validate each candidate against schema, extension, runtime invariants, and objectives. Deterministic puzzle claims require replayable solution witnesses or a sound exhaustive result; stochastic games require honest sampled success and uncertainty. TIMEOUT and RESOURCE_LIMIT are unknown outcomes, not UNSOLVABLE. The generator must not hand a hidden answer to the evaluation player.

Measure topology, mechanic use, solution/action structure, and strategy differences. Different IDs, seeds, or hashes alone do not show diversity. Normalize rotations/reflections where the rules treat them as equivalent.

For a campaign, check mechanic dependencies and exposure before combination. Use introduction, practice, combine, twist, pressure, mastery, rest, and final assessment only where they serve the audience. Track coverage, repetition, spacing, difficulty spikes, and similarity. A sequence of monotonically larger parameters is not sufficient curriculum evidence.
